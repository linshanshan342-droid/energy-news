01 Largest Crude Importer +
Market Context

China surpassed the United States as the world's largest crude oil importer in 2017 and has maintained that position ever since, importing over 10 million barrels per day. This structural demand shapes global refining margins, tanker routes, and producer pricing strategies across the Middle East, Russia, West Africa, and the Americas.

02 Refining & Petrochemical Hub +
Industry Dynamics

China is home to the world's largest refining capacity, with over 18 million barrels per day of crude processing capability. The expansion of mega-refineries and integrated petrochemical complexes is reshaping global product markets, from gasoline and diesel to aromatics and polymers.

03 LNG & Gas Demand Driver +
Structural Shift

China became the world's largest LNG importer in 2021, surpassing Japan. Gas demand continues to grow as coal-to-gas switching accelerates under the country's dual-carbon targets. Long-term LNG contracts and pipeline gas from Russia are reshaping global gas trade flows.

04 Asia Trade Flow Anchor +
Geopolitical Significance

China's position as the anchor of Asian trade flows means its economic performance, policy decisions, and geopolitical relationships have outsized impacts on regional and global commodity markets, supply chains, and capital allocation strategies.
